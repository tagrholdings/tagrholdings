Why we built it

Three jobs that used to live in three different places

Before the CRM, finding deals meant browsing broker sites by hand, deal status lived in email threads, and each company's EOS lived in its own spreadsheet. Now it's one system, with one login and the same data everywhere.

Before we start

Words you'll see

Most of the vocabulary comes from M&A or from EOS, the operating system our companies run on.

Lead

A company we might buy or work with, on the Leads board, moving through stages until it closes.

Sourcing

Where deals are found before we work them: the engine's finds, listing emails and anything added by hand wait in Review.

Buy box / Fit

What we want to buy (industry, area, revenue, profit, price). Each find gets a Fit badge against it.

Workspace

One company. Its own people, data and inbox address. One login can belong to several.

EOS

The Entrepreneurial Operating System: a simple, proven way to run a company — vision, people, data, issues, process, traction.

Rock

A 90-day priority with one owner and a clear finish line. 3–7 per quarter.

Scorecard

The 5–15 numbers that say how the company is doing, entered every week or month.

L10 · IDS

The weekly 90-minute leadership meeting. Its core is IDS: Identify, Discuss, Solve the top issues.

  • Two more you'll meet: the V/TO (Vision/Traction Organizer, the company's plan on one page) and a To-Do (a 7-day commitment from the L10, kept in Activities).

Sourcing → Search profiles

Search profiles

A saved description of what we want to buy and where. The engine runs every active profile on its schedule.

What you set

  • Industries and keywords: HVAC, air conditioning, heating and cooling
  • City, state and radius, plus how strict location must be
  • Sources: Google Places, Brave Search, broker listing sites
  • Buy box: revenue, profit, max asking price, signal words like “retiring”
  • Cap and frequency: max new leads per run, how often it runs

Key point

  • A new industry or region is a form to fill in, never new code. Run now starts a search right away.
Example

How the lead engine works

Four doors in, one review queue

Whatever the source, every find goes through the same steps (dedupe, AI extraction, fit score) and lands in the same Review queue for a person to decide.

Door 1 · Scheduled

Broker websites

About once a week it discovers brokers in the state, then opens each one's “buy a business” pages the way a person would, following industry categories and next-page links.

Door 1 · Scheduled

Google Places & Brave

Finds operating companies in the area (name, address, phone, website), for owners we could approach before they list.

Door 2 · Email

Listing emails

Each workspace has its own inbox address. Broker digests sent there are read by AI: one email with 5 businesses becomes 5 leads.

Doors 3 & 4 · You

Paste, share, API

Paste a link or text, or tap Share → TAGR CRM on your phone. Scripts can use a secured API.

DEDUPE

Seen before? Skipped, so it costs nothing. The same listing by email and on the site becomes one lead.

→
AI EXTRACTION

Price, revenue, profit, employees, years, reason for selling, contact. Values kept exactly as written.

→
FIT + LOCATION

Scored against the profile's buy box and area. Badges only, nothing is hidden.

→
REVIEW

A person decides: add to pipeline, or dismiss. Dismissed finds never come back.

  • Four doors: scheduled (broker sites + Google/Brave), email, by hand or shared from the phone, and an API.
  • The engine respects robots.txt and captchas and never works around a block. A site that blocks it is flagged for a person to check.
  • If the AI step fails, nothing is lost: the find is saved with its raw text and marked.
CRM → Sourcing → Review

Review

The queue for everything the engine, an email or a teammate brought in. Nothing here is in the pipeline yet. A person decides.

What you do here

  • Open a find: details, source, original link
  • Add to pipeline: it becomes a card on the Leads board, with contact and organization created for you
  • Dismiss: archived as history; the engine won't bring it back
  • Sort by best fit and filter by when it was found

The two badges

  • Fit: Match / Partial / Miss / Unknown, measured against the buy box
  • Location: In area / Region / Same state / Location?

Review → open a find

What the AI reads out of a listing

Opening a find shows the fields the AI extracted, exactly as the source wrote them, and a line-by-line check against the buy box.

Extracted for every find

  • Business, industry, summary, location, website
  • Asking price, revenue, profit, employees, years in business
  • Reason for selling and signals (“retiring”, “owner selling”)
  • Seller or broker contact: name, email, phone

Safety

  • The AI is told not to guess or convert. Links are only trusted if they literally appear in the source.

Sourcing → Email sources

Email sources

Some brokers never let a program read their site, but they'll email new listings. This page tracks which of those lists our inbox address is subscribed to.

How it works

  • Each workspace has its own inbox address. Sign the broker's list up with it.
  • Simple forms (no captcha) can be filled in by the engine, with our name, phone and company
  • When the “confirm your subscription” email arrives, the CRM clicks the link for us and marks it subscribed
  • Captcha, NDA or account required → flagged to do by hand, then Mark subscribed

Engine spend: what the automation costs

Every paid call (Google, Brave, the AI, email) is logged the moment it happens and added up per service and per run.

  • Which profiles are worth their cost
  • How many leads per run make sense (lower the cap or the frequency)
  • Spot a failing run (e.g. a Google quota hit) the same day

Good to know

  • Figures are estimates from public list prices, not invoices. Good for comparing, not for reconciling.

Security & trust

Each company's data stays with that company

Isolation is enforced by the database itself, not just by the screens. That matters as more companies join.

Isolation at the database

Every record belongs to one workspace, and the database blocks reading another company's rows even if a screen had a bug. Every request re-checks access.

Least privilege for the engine

The lead engine connects with its own restricted role: it can add finds, never read contacts or deals, never delete, never cross companies.

Errors never leak

Users see clear, friendly messages; internal database errors never reach the browser.

Invitation-only access

No public sign-up. One-time invite links that expire. Roles per workspace.

Untrusted input stays untrusted

Scraped and AI-written data is treated as untrusted: links validated, signed webhooks verified, the API rate-limited.

Polite, legal sourcing

robots.txt and captchas are respected, never bypassed. Costs are logged per call, and runs are capped.

  • In business terms: even if a screen had a bug, the database itself won't let one company see another's data.
  • That's what makes it safe to put different companies (Tagr, Grounded, Menlo) on the same platform.
